    Motion Retargeting for Humanoids¶

    Motion retargeting is the process of transferring motion from one body to another — taking movement captured from a source (human, robot, or character) and making it work naturally on a target with a different morphology.

    The Core Problem¶

    No two bodies are identical. A motion that looks natural on a 6ft human looks wrong on a short robot with stubby arms. Retargeting must preserve motion intent while respecting the target's physical constraints.

    Source body (motion capture) ──→ [Retargeting] ──→ Target humanoid robot
  different proportions                               must look natural +
  different DOFs                                      be physically valid

    What Makes It Hard¶

    Morphological mismatch

    Different limb lengths, joint placements, degrees of freedom (DOF), and range of motion limits. A robot's shoulder may have 3 DOF where a human has 6.

    Kinematic constraints

    A robot may not be able to fully extend a limb the way a human can, or may lack wrist roll entirely.

    Dynamic feasibility

    A motion valid for a human may be unstable or torque-infeasible for a robot — different mass distribution, foot size, actuator limits.

    Contact preservation

    Foot contact with the ground, hand grasps, or object interactions must remain semantically intact after retargeting.

    Types of Retargeting¶

    Transfers joint angles or end-effector positions without considering physics.

    Pros: Fast, simple to implement
    Cons: Can produce floating feet, ground penetration, unstable poses
    Use when: Physical accuracy isn't critical (animation, visualization, data filtering)

    Solves for motions that are dynamically feasible — the robot can actually execute them without falling.

    • Uses simulation (MuJoCo, Isaac Lab / IsaacGym)
    • Requires trajectory optimization or reinforcement learning
    • Output motions are realizable on real hardware
      Much harder — but necessary for deployment.

    A neural network learns the mapping from source to target motion space.

    • Can generalize across many motions
    • Trained with imitation learning or adversarial objectives
    • Examples: SMPL → robot joint angles via learned regression (PHC, UHC, SONIC)

    Common Pipeline¶

    1. Source motion    →  MoCap data or SMPL parameters (β shape, θ pose)
2. Skeleton mapping →  Match source joints to target joints (by name or IK)
3. IK / optimization → Solve target joint angles to match key poses / end-effectors
4. Contact handling →  Fix foot sliding, enforce ground contact (contact-invariant opt.)
5. Physics check    →  Ensure balance, torque limits, collision-free
6. Post-processing  →  Smooth trajectories, filter noise

    Key Challenges¶

    Challenge Description
    Foot contact Preventing sliding or floating during locomotion
    Self-collision Robot hitting itself mid-motion
    Torque limits Joints can't produce arbitrary force
    Underactuation Some joints aren't independently controllable
    Whole-body balance Center of mass must stay over support polygon

    Retargeting in the RL Context¶

    Modern humanoid control (Tesla Optimus, Figure, Unitree H1/G1) uses retargeting as a reference motion provider for reinforcement learning:

    Human MoCap → Retarget to robot morphology → Use as RL reference trajectory
                                             → Robot learns to track it
                                             → Generalizes to real hardware

    Frameworks like PHC, AMP (Adversarial Motion Priors), and ASE rely heavily on this pipeline — retargeted SMPL motions become the "teacher" the RL policy imitates.

    SONIC (notes) takes this further: its L_token + L_cycle losses align human SMPL and robot motion into a shared token space, enabling zero-shot human→robot transfer without any explicit retargeting at test time.

    Notable Tools & Papers¶

    Tool / Paper What it does
    MoSh / MoSh++ Fits SMPL body model to raw MoCap markers
    GMR Gaussian Motion Retargeting — used in SONIC's pipeline
    PyRoki Differentiable robot kinematics for retargeting
    PHC (Perpetual Humanoid Control) SMPL-based retargeting + RL for physics humanoids
    UHC (Universal Humanoid Control) Cross-embodiment retargeting + motion imitation
    HUMOR Probabilistic motion model for robust retargeting under noise
    AMP (Peng et al.) Adversarial Motion Priors — RL with retargeted MoCap as discriminator
    ASE Adversarial Skill Embeddings — latent skill space from retargeted data
    Isaac Lab / MuJoCo MJX GPU-accelerated simulators where retargeted motions are executed

    Relevance to DreamVu¶

    • EXP-001 uses GR00T with BEHAVIOR_R1_PRO embodiment — retargeted SMPL data from BEHAVIOR-1K is the training source
    • SONIC's GMR + PyRoki retargeting pipeline is a practical starting point for DreamVu's own data → Unitree G1 pipeline
    • EgoDex (notes) collected 829 hrs of human hand motion — bridging the embodiment gap (human hand → robot end-effector) is effectively a retargeting problem
    • Understanding contact preservation is critical before running real-robot manipulation experiments

    Open Questions / Tasks¶

    • Evaluate GMR vs. PyRoki retargeting quality on DreamVu's robot morphology
    • Test PHC-retargeted SMPL clips as reference trajectories in Isaac Lab
    • Investigate whether SONIC's L_token + L_cycle approach can replace explicit retargeting for our use case
    • Benchmark foot contact quality: kinematic vs. physics-based retargeting on pick-and-place tasks
