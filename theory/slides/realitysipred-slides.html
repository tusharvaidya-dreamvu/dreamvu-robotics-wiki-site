What Is a Gaussian Splat?

3 / 34

3D Gaussian Splatting, in Plain Terms

A relatively new scene representation (2023) — worth grounding before anything else.

  • A real scene is represented as millions of tiny 3D Gaussians (soft, fuzzy blobs), each with a position, orientation/scale (an ellipsoid), a color, and an opacity
  • Trained from ordinary posed photos/video — no depth sensor or LiDAR required, though extra sensors help (see next section)
  • Rendered with a fast, differentiable rasterizer — new camera viewpoints can be synthesized in real time, at photorealistic quality
  • Not a mesh: no fixed topology, no polygons — this is exactly what makes it fast and photoreal, and exactly what makes physics/robotics use cases harder (more on this later)
Think of it as the representation between a photo and a 3D model — it looks and moves like real 3D, but doesn't have the structure a game engine or physics engine expects.

What We're Trying to Do

realitySipred — the name is a nod to 'reality': turning captured reality into something usable downstream.

  • Convert real-world captures into usable 3D assets — not just a pretty viewer scene
  • Primary consumer: robotics data generation and training — synthetic-but-grounded-in-reality rollouts
  • Secondary consumers: other simulation use cases wherever a real environment needs to become a simulatable one
The bar is not 'does it look good' — it's 'can a robot policy be trained or evaluated against it.'
6 / 34

Two-Phase Structure

Phase 1 — Capture & Generate

  • Get a convincing, high-fidelity Gaussian Splat from real-world capture
  • Handle the practical mess: rectification, dynamic objects, blur, sync
  • Explore every sensor modality the capture rig offers, not just RGB

Phase 2 — Sim-Ready Conversion

  • Turn a static splat into an asset that can be perturbed and interacted with
  • Add physics-based manipulation directly on the splat representation
  • Generate diverse, varied training data from one real capture

The Alia Pro as a Splat-Generation Advantage

Most Gaussian Splat pipelines in the wild are built on RGB-only image sets. We don't have to be.

  • Alia Pro captures stereo depth, IMU, and multi-camera rig geometry alongside RGB — all in one device
  • These aren't just "extra data" — they're strong priors that a pure-RGB pipeline has to instead recover the hard way (structure-from-motion alone, no ground truth scale or gravity reference)
  • Rig extrinsics are known by construction, not estimated — a meaningfully different starting point than a single moving camera
Every modality below is something a competitor's RGB-only rig doesn't have access to at all.

Relighting — The Hardest Open Problem

Not unique to us — this is an unsolved industry-wide problem today.

  • A splat bakes in the lighting/shading of the environment it was captured in
  • Insert a foreign object (any asset not captured in that same lighting) and it visibly looks "alien" — its shading doesn't match the scene's light sources
  • No robust general relighting solution exists in the field today — this constrains any "insert a new object into a real scene" workflow, not just ours
This is a real, current limitation to be upfront about — not something we expect to solve incidentally as a side effect of other work.

Splats → Simulation-Ready Assets

The Phase 2 Problem

Phase 1 gets a good-looking splat. Phase 2 makes it usable for data generation.

  • Goal: capture data sets across all the variation/perturbation a real-world capture alone can't give you
  • First working capability: physics-based manipulation on the splat itself (EXP-007) — an object falls, bounces, picks up spin, directly composited into the real captured splat world
  • This validates the direction: physics does not require first converting the scene to a mesh
21 / 34

Splat-World vs. Mesh-World for Physics

Splat-Based Physics (our primary bet)

  • No lossy splat→mesh conversion step needed
  • Keeps the real captured visual fidelity throughout
  • Rigid-body-proxy transform reapplication is cheap and already demonstrated working
  • Newer ground — less tooling, but also less IP/competitive lock-in against us

Mesh-Based Physics (secondary offering)

  • Industry-standard — mature physics engines, asset pipelines, Isaac/Omniverse tooling
  • Heavily dominated by existing players and their IP — hard to differentiate on
  • Still useful and requested by some customers — kept ready, not abandoned
  • Explored in parallel (EXP-006): RANSAC plane fits, convex-hull slabs, USD export
22 / 34

The Strategic Call

  • This is a product-positioning decision as much as a technical one — worth surfacing explicitly, not just letting the tech direction imply it
  • Both pipelines share upstream capture/sensor work (Phase 1) — this is not a duplicated effort, just a fork at the asset-conversion stage
Keep splat-based physics-manipulation as our **primary product direction** — it's where the competitive landscape is least dominated by incumbent IP. Keep the mesh-based pipeline maintained and ready as a **secondary offering** for customers who specifically need it.

Phase 2

Next Challenge: Non-Rigid Objects

24 / 34

Rigid-Body-Proxy Isn't Enough on Its Own

EXP-007 validates rigid motion (falls, bounces, collides) directly on a splat. Real-world manipulation scenes are not all rigid.

  • The rigid-body-proxy formula reapplies one transform to every Gaussian in an object — correct for a rigid ball or box, wrong for anything that bends, folds, or stretches
  • Cloth, soft/deformable produce, flexible tooling, and general non-rigid manipulation targets are common in real robotics scenes — this is a core gap, not an edge case, for making splat-based physics feel intuitive and close to real-world behavior
  • Closing this gap is what actually determines whether Phase 2 output is broadly useful for manipulation data generation, not just rigid pick-and-place
**4D Gaussian Splatting (4D-GS)** — Gaussians extended with a learned time-varying/deformation component — is the accurate answer here: it models non-rigid motion natively inside the splat representation itself, rather than bolting a separate mesh/cage-and-skinning step on top of a static splat.

API / Microservices Release Plan

31 / 34

Principle: Services, Not Shared Scripts

How the team consumes each other's work, day to day.

  • We will not share raw code between team members to rerun locally — every capability is consumed as an internally-hosted API, not a script someone else has to clone and re-execute
  • One hosted service per capability means one source of truth — no drift between "whoever last edited the script" copies of the same pipeline
  • Onboarding a new team member (or a new consuming team) means pointing them at an API, not walking them through someone else's experiment repo
This turns EXP-005/006/007's experiment scripts into productized internal services, not one-off research code.
