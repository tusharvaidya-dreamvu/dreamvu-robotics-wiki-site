The Problem We're Proposing to Solve

A retail humanoid must perform tasks humans do naturally — but motion data from humans cannot be directly used to control a robot.

Human Side — What We Have

  • Retail workers & shoppers performing natural tasks: reach shelves, grasp products, navigate aisles, place items in basket
  • Video + MoCap data: AMASS, EMHI, SAGA, DreamVu SABER (MediaPipe + HaMeR)
  • Rich, diverse motion — but recorded in human body coordinates, not robot joint space
  • Human body: soft ROM limits, ~24 joints (SMPL), no hard stops

Robot Side — What We Need

  • Unitree G1 performing equivalent retail tasks autonomously and safely
  • 43 actuated DoF, hard joint limits, different link proportions
  • Needs joint-angle commands at 30–50 Hz from a closed-loop learned policy
  • Must be physically stable, safe around humans, and sim2real transferable

Main Hurdles in Retargeting

Six technical barriers that make human → robot motion transfer non-trivial:

  • Morphological mismatch — Human and robot have different limb lengths, joint placements, and body proportions. The same joint angles produce completely different end-effector positions on each body — you cannot simply copy angles across.
  • Hard joint limits — A human's ROM is soft (biology limits motion gradually). A robot's servo has an absolute mechanical stop — exceeding it damages hardware or causes simulation to become unstable.
  • Physics feasibility — A retargeted pose may look correct but be dynamically unstable: the robot's center of mass (COM) falls outside its support polygon and it topples. Static validity ≠ dynamic stability.
  • Contact artifacts — Naive IK retargeting introduces foot sliding (planted foot drifts sideways), ground penetration (joints go below floor), and self-collision (arm intersects torso) — none acceptable on real hardware.
  • Sim-to-real gap — The RL policy trains in GPU simulation (IsaacGym). Real-robot dynamics differ: sensor noise, motor latency, unmodeled floor friction. The policy must be robust enough to transfer zero-shot.
  • Retail-specific constraints — Variable reach height (0.3–1.8 m), narrow aisles (0.9 m), low-friction tile (μ ≈ 0.4), shelf collision avoidance during arm approach, and graceful failure/recovery — all absent from standard RL benchmarks.

Three-Layer Dataset Strategy

No single retail-specific MoCap dataset exists — compose from four complementary sources, including DreamVu's own SABER data:

Layer A
AMASS — Locomotion in constrained spaces
Unifies 15 optical MoCap datasets into SMPL framework. 40+ hrs, 300+ subjects, 11,000+ motions. Filter for walking with stops, turns, direction changes — aisle navigation base.
Layer B
EMHI — Egocentric manipulation (shelf reach)
885 sequences, 58 subjects, 39 actions (~28.5 hrs). VR headset + body IMUs, SMPL annotations. First-person perspective precisely matching retail shelf-reach scenarios.
Layer C
SAGA + Motion-X — Whole-body object interaction
Stochastic whole-body grasping + Motion-X. Diversity score 13.17 vs HumanML3D's 9.84. SMPL-X format with hand-level detail. Pick-up, carry, place — product grasping coverage.
Layer D
DreamVu SABER — In-house retail capture (proprietary)
Egocentric + third-person retail videos processed through DreamVu's SABER pipeline. MediaPipe Hands extracts 21-joint hand landmarks (world-space metres, 30 Hz). HaMeR (HAWOR) recovers dense hand mesh + wrist 6-DoF pose, closing the gap MediaPipe leaves on wrist orientation. Only dataset natively aligned to DreamVu's target scenes, shelf geometries, and Unitree G1 embodiment — see EXP-002.

Retail-Specific Annotation Strategy

Segment clips by action primitive using text labels (HumanML3D text-motion pairing framework):

  • Walk toward shelf — aisle navigation, stopping, turning in ~0.9–1.2 m wide corridors
  • Reach arm up to shelf — upper-body extension to 1.8 m height, balance on low-friction tile (μ ≈ 0.4)
  • Grasp object at chest height — mid-shelf reach, fine motor contact
  • Place item in basket / cart — transfer motion, hold with grasp state
  • Squat to low shelf — whole-body crouch to 0.3 m, then recover and walk
  • Lateral step — sideways movement in narrow aisles without full turn
Key insight: shelf height range 0.3 m → 1.8 m drives the full upper-body ROM requirement — more demanding than most RL locomotion benchmarks.

IK-Based Retargeting Formulation

Per-frame optimization over robot joint space, minimizing weighted keypoint error:

q̃ᵗᴿ = argmin Σk∈K wₖ ‖ pᵏᴿ(qᴿ) − α · pᵏᴴ(qᵗᴴ) ‖² + λₛ ‖q̇ᴿ‖² (smoothness) + λ_c φ_contact(qᴿ) (foot contact consistency) subject to qᴿ ∈ C_R (joint limits + self-collision constraints)
  • K = key-point set: wrists, ankles, head, COM — wrists weighted highest for retail reach accuracy
  • α = proportional scale factor between human and robot link lengths
  • φ_contact = contact consistency — foot on ground ↔ human foot on ground
  • C_R = robot joint limits + self-collision mesh constraints

Learned Retargeting Approaches

GCN Retargeting Network

  • Graph conv. network learns SMPL → robot joint mapping
  • Enables upper-body reach while lower body maintains balance
  • Generalizes across clip diversity better than per-frame IK
  • Creates motion dataset for RL imitation policy

ReActor / "Retargeting Matters"

  • Closes the retarget→RL loop: retarget, simulate, measure artifact severity
  • Re-optimizes retargeting itself before RL training
  • Motion Difficulty Score quantifies tracking hardness per clip
  • Enables curriculum ordering — easy shelf heights first
Standard practice leaves retargeting artifacts in the reference and lets RL correct them — ReActor approach is cleaner: fix artifacts upstream, then train RL on physically feasible references.

Pre-RL Feasibility Filter

Run retargeted trajectory τ̃ᴿ through four processing steps before any RL training:

  • Foot contact annotation — classify each frame: left contact, right contact, double stance, flight. Retail motion is mostly double-support with occasional single-support during turns and lateral steps.
  • Ground penetration removal — project any joint position below floor plane back to floor + ε. Critical for tile floors with no compliance.
  • Self-collision resolution — run SMPL SDF or robot URDF collision mesh checks; re-solve IK where penetration detected. Arm-torso intersection common during full-height shelf reach.
  • Motion Difficulty Score — quantifies how hard each trajectory is for RL imitation. Higher score = higher expected tracking error. Use to filter or curriculum-order retail clips before training.
Output: a clean reference dataset τ̃ᴿ_clean where every frame is physically feasible — RL policy only needs to learn tracking, not physics correction.

Action Space — PD Target Joint Positions

Output: desired joint positions, converted to torques via PD controller on real hardware:

aₜ ∈ ℝⁿᴰᵒᶠ (43 dims for Unitree G1) Torque: τ = kₚ(aₜ − qₜ) − k_d q̇ₜ
  • Why PD targets, not raw torques: high-level actions → joint-level torques through whole-body control, reducing RL dimensionality and addressing sim-to-real via safe trajectory generation
  • kₚ and k_d tuning: matched to real robot servo gains — critical for sim-to-real transfer
  • Action repeat: policy runs at 30–50 Hz; PD controller runs at hardware rate (1000 Hz)
  • Action clipping: ±0.5 rad delta from current pose prevents large discontinuous jumps

Reward Function — Three Components

rₜ = w_imit · r_imit + w_task · r_task + w_reg · r_reg Imitation reward (motion style fidelity): r_imit = exp(−Σₖ αₖ ‖pᵏᴿ − p̃ᵏᴿ‖²) · exp(−β‖q̇ᴿ − q̃̇ᴿ‖²) ↑ wrist αₖ weighted 3× higher for retail reach accuracy Regularization penalty: r_reg = −λₑ‖τ‖² − λⱼ|q̈| − λ_f · r_foot_slide
DeepMimic-style composition: imitation keeps the motion human-like, task reward drives goal completion, regularization prevents energy-wasteful or jerky behavior on real hardware.

Algorithm: PPO + Privileged Teacher-Student

Teacher Policy (Training)

  • Full privileged state — known shelf map + object positions
  • PPO with GAE, 4096+ parallel envs in IsaacGym
  • Reference State Initialization (RSI) — start episodes from mid-skill states
  • Delta action space relative to reference trajectory
  • VIRAL architecture: long-horizon loco-manipulation

Student Policy (Deployment)

  • Depth image + proprioception only (real robot input)
  • Distilled from teacher via DAgger / behavior cloning
  • Zero-shot transfer to real hardware
  • VIRAL achieves 54 continuous loco-manipulation cycles zero-shot
  • Diverse spatial + appearance variations covered
Teacher knows everything; student knows only what the robot can sense. Gap is closed by large-scale simulation diversity — not domain randomization alone.

Skill Decomposition for Retail

High-Level Planner (STRIPS / LLM task planner) ↓ Skill Library ① Walk-to-shelf (nav primitive) ② Reach-low (squat + arm extend) ③ Reach-mid (stand + arm extend) ④ Reach-high (tiptoe + arm raise) ⑤ Grasp-product (finger closure) ⑥ Place-in-basket (transfer + release) ⑦ Lateral-step (narrow aisle)

Training Strategy

  • Each primitive trained separately, then blended
  • HOMIE framework: locomotion that enables walking + squatting while robust to upper-body arm disturbances
  • Curriculum learning: easy shelf heights first (mid), then extremes (low squat, high tiptoe)
  • RSI: start episodes from mid-skill states — not just standing — to learn transitions
  • Hardest transition: walk → decelerate → squat/reach → grasp → stand → walk

Three Hard Problems Specific to Retail

  • Variable reach height with balance — Reaching a 1.8 m shelf while standing on low-friction tile (μ ≈ 0.4) is dynamically very different from reaching a 0.5 m shelf while squatting. Most RL benchmarks don't cover this height range under simultaneous locomotion perturbation. Requires dedicated curriculum across the full 0.3–1.8 m range.
  • Shelf collision avoidance during manipulation — The arm must not sweep products off adjacent shelves during the reach approach. Requires a shaped penalty active during the reach phase trajectory, not just at final-state success. Standard motion imitation RL misses this without explicit shelf geometry in the reward.
  • Graceful failure and replanning — If a grasp fails (product slides, hand misaligns), the policy must back off and retry without falling. Standard motion imitation RL has no recovery behavior; requires a task-level recovery policy layer or an explicit retry skill in the hierarchy.
